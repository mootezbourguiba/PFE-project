Chapter 1

Introduction and System Context

1.1 General Context
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) have become essential tools in modern aerospace
applications, including surveillance, environmental monitoring, and infrastructure in-
spection. However, the aviation industry relies heavily on the reliability and avail-
ability of avionics and propulsion systems. Unexpected in-flight failures can lead to
catastrophic crashes, operational disruptions, and severe safety risks.

1.2 Problem Statement
Modern aircraft generate large volumes of operational data. Traditionally, maintenance
is performed either reactively (after a failure) or preventively (on a strict time schedule,
which is often inefficient). To maximize UAV safety, there is a critical need to transition
to Predictive Maintenance—leveraging real-time telemetry data to detect abnormal
behaviors before critical failures occur.

Because an entire UAV contains thousands of components, this project focuses
specifically on the Propulsion System (Brushless DC Motor and ESC). The
motor is the most critical mechanical component of a fixed-wing UAV. Slow mechanical
degradation, such as bearing wear, generates measurable precursors (e.g., increased
electrical current, rising temperatures, and vibrations) making it the ideal candidate
for predictive analysis.

1.3 Project Objectives
The objective of this project is to develop a modular and scalable software platform
capable of:

• Designing a conceptual UAV platform to serve as the physical baseline.

• Simulating and acquiring avionics time-series data (Current, Voltage, Tempera-
ture).

• Detecting abnormal motor degradation patterns using Machine Learning anomaly
detection.

• Visualizing the motor’s health status through an interactive web dashboard.

Scrum Project Management

2.1 Agile Methodology Overview
To organize the development process of the proposed system, the Scrum agile method-
ology was adopted instead of a traditional waterfall approach. Scrum relies on an
iterative and incremental development cycle, emphasizing continuous adaptation and
progressive delivery of functional components.

2.2 Identification of Actors
Before defining the system functionalities, it is essential to identify the actors who will
interact with the system and specify the actions they can perform.

• Maintenance Engineer: The primary user of the application. They use the
system to ensure the daily monitoring of the drone fleet. The platform must
allow them to:

– Consult the real-time telemetry data (Voltage, Current, Temperature).
– View the AI-generated "Health Score" of the motor.
– Receive predictive alerts regarding impending bearing failures.
– Consult the historical data of previous flights for post-mission analysis.

• Drone Operator: A secondary user actively flying the UAV. The platform must
allow them to:

– Receive instantaneous critical alerts if an anomaly is detected mid-flight.
– Make emergency landing decisions based on the AI’s early warnings.

2.3 Identification of System Needs
Based on the actor analysis, the system requirements are classified into functional and
non-functional needs.

2.3.1 Functional Requirements
The system must provide the following functionalities:

• Data Ingestion: Read and process MAVLink/Telemetry time-series data.

• Anomaly Detection: Run machine learning algorithms to detect deviations in
the Current-Temperature relationship.

• Dashboarding: Display interactive charts and motor health metrics.

• Alerting: Trigger visual warnings when a degradation pattern is identified.

2.3.2 Non-Functional Requirements
The system must respect the following constraints:

• Performance: The system must process time-series data and run AI predictions
with minimal latency.

• Usability: The dashboard must offer a simple, intuitive, and highly visual in-
terface for the engineers.

• Reliability: The system must minimize false negatives (missing a real failure)
to ensure drone safety.

• Maintainability: The codebase must be modular, primarily using Python, to
allow future integration of new AI models.

3.2 UAV Configuration Selection
After evaluating various aerodynamic layouts, a Blended Wing Body (BWB) /
Flying Wing configuration with a rear-mounted pusher motor was selected. This
advanced configuration offers significant advantages for avionics monitoring:

• Avionics Payload Space: The thick central fuselage pod provides a large in-
ternal volume to house the intelligent monitoring platform, flight controller, and
batteries.

• Aerodynamic Efficiency: Eliminating the traditional tail assembly reduces
drag and structural weight, increasing flight endurance.

4.3 Simulation Methodology and Physics
To train a predictive maintenance algorithm, the simulation must generate both "Healthy"
and "Degrading" flight data. The primary failure mode modeled is motor bearing

14



degradation.
The simulation logic follows physical electrical laws:

1. Friction: As bearings wear out, mechanical friction increases.

2. Current Draw: To overcome this friction and maintain a constant cruise speed
of 5000 RPM, the Electronic Speed Controller (ESC) must supply more electrical
current (I) to the motor.

3. Thermal Dynamics: Electrical heat generation in the stator coils is propor-
tional to the square of the current (Ploss = I2R). Therefore, as the current rises
to fight friction, the motor temperature rises exponentially.

Using Python, six 60-minute flights were simulated (1 data point per second).
Flights 1 through 5 represent healthy baseline operations, while Flight 6 introduces
an exponential friction multiplier to simulate an impending bearing failure.

4.4 Simulation Results
The resulting telemetry dataset (uav_motor_telemetry.csv) successfully captures the
physical degradation of the motor.

Figure 4.3 illustrates the electrical current draw. During the healthy flights, the
current remains stable at approximately 10 Amps. However, during the degrading
flight, the current steadily climbs toward 14 Amps to compensate for the simulated
friction.

Chapter 5

Sprint 2: Predictive Maintenance AI
Engine

5.1 Sprint Objective
This Sprint focuses on the core artificial intelligence component of the platform. Using
the time-series data generated in Sprint 1, an anomaly detection algorithm was im-
plemented to identify early signs of motor bearing degradation before a catastrophic
in-flight failure occurs.

5.2 Algorithm Selection: Isolation Forest
For this predictive maintenance task, the Isolation Forest algorithm (from the scikit-
learn library) was selected. Unlike simple static thresholds (e.g., triggering an alarm
only when temperature exceeds 65◦C), Isolation Forest is an unsupervised machine
learning algorithm that excels at detecting anomalies in multi-dimensional time-series
data. It works by isolating observations by randomly selecting a feature and then
randomly selecting a split value. Because anomalies are "few and different," they are
isolated closer to the root of the decision trees, allowing for rapid detection of subtle
drifts in the Current-Temperature relationship.

5.3 Model Training and Execution
The AI model was trained exclusively on the baseline data from Flights 1 through
5. During this phase, the algorithm learned the multidimensional boundaries of a
"Healthy" motor operating at a cruise speed of 5000 RPM.

Once trained, the model was tested against the telemetry data of Flight 6, which
contained the simulated exponential bearing friction.

5.4 Results and Early Warning Evaluation
The Isolation Forest successfully detected the onset of mechanical degradation long
before critical thermal limits were reached.

17



As shown in Figure 5.1, the algorithm flagged the first anomaly at approximately t =
161 seconds. At this point, the motor temperature was still within nominal operating
bounds (≈ 51◦C), meaning a traditional threshold alarm would not have triggered.

Figure 5.1: Isolation Forest Anomaly Detection on Degrading Motor Telemetry

By detecting the micro-deviations in the electrical current draw caused by the initial
bearing wear, the AI provided the Drone Operator with over 3,400 seconds (approx.
57 minutes) of early warning. This successful prediction validates the core objective
of the Intelligent Avionics Health Monitoring Platform: enabling proactive maintenance
and preventing catastrophic in-flight failures.

Chapter 7

Conclusion

This report documented the complete development of an Intelligent Avionics Health
Monitoring and Predictive Maintenance Platform. By adopting the Agile
Scrum methodology, the project was systematically executed through four distinct
phases, moving from conceptual design to a fully functional AI-powered prototype.

The project achieved three major technical objectives:

1. Physical Modeling: The Blended Wing Body UAV was mathematically sized
and validated as a physical baseline.

2. Digital Twin Simulation: A physics-aware simulation was created, providing a
dataset that successfully replicated the precursor signals of a mechanical failure.

3. Predictive Intelligence: The Isolation Forest model demonstrated its ability
to detect anomalies at an early stage, providing over 50 minutes of early warning
in simulated scenarios.

The final dashboard integrates these components into a unified interface, proving
that machine learning can be effectively utilized to prevent catastrophic in-flight failures
in the aerospace industry.

Future Perspectives: The modular nature of this platform allows for future
integration with real MAVLink data streams. Furthermore, the anomaly detection
engine could be expanded to monitor other critical avionics components, such as battery
health or flight controller vibration patterns.
